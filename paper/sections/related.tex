\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Video-to-audio generation.}
Early work introduced Greatest Hits with example-based synthesis \citep{owens2016visually} and VEGAS with direct waveform generation \citep{zhou2018visual}. \citet{chen2020regnet} introduced VAS and pointed out that many sounds in a video have no visible source. SpecVQGAN \citep{iashin2021taming}, our base model, adapts VQGAN \citep{esser2021taming} to spectrograms: a codebook plus a transformer that predicts codes from visual features. CondFoley \citep{du2023conditional} uses a similar code model and re-ranks samples with a synchronization model at test time. Diff-Foley \citep{luo2023difffoley} guides diffusion sampling with an alignment classifier, and Seeing and Hearing \citep{xing2024seeing} steers pretrained generators with a frozen multimodal aligner. Other systems improve alignment through architecture or visual features \citep{mei2024foleygen,wang2024frieren,viertola2025vaura,cheng2025mmaudio,mo2025foleyflow}, condition on a timing signal predicted from video such as onsets, energy or a coarse layout \citep{comunita2024syncfusion,zhang2026foleycrafter,ton2025taro,jeong2025rewas,wang2024tiva}, or post-train with timing-aware preference pairs \citep{chan2026v2adpo,cheng2026syncdpo}. Semantic conditioning without a timing mechanism is also common \citep{sheffer2023im2wav,wang2024v2amapper}. PAL differs in where the synchronization signal enters: as a differentiable training loss from a frozen critic on the generator's own output.

\paragraph{Synchronization models.}
SyncNet \citep{chung2016outoftime} learned a two-stream embedding for lip sync. \citet{owens2018audio} learned audio-visual features by detecting shifted audio, the supervision our critic uses, and \citet{chung2019perfect} trained with a softmax over many candidate offsets, the form of our critic objective. Later models handle lips and voices \citep{kadandale2022vocalist,chen2024mtdvocalist}, localization \citep{afouras2020self}, semantic correspondence \citep{arandjelovic2018objects} and open-domain video with sparse cues \citep{iashin2022sparse,iashin2024synchformer}; MMAudio's DeSync metric is computed with Synchformer \citep{iashin2024synchformer,cheng2025mmaudio}.

\paragraph{Synchronization losses inside generators.}
Wav2Lip \citep{prajwal2020wav2lip} pretrains a sync expert on real video, freezes it, and trains a face generator with the expert's negative log synchronization probability. VCA-GAN \citep{kim2021vcagan} adds a contrastive synchronization term to a lip-to-speech generator. \citet{niu2023synchronization} train a lip-to-speech generator with a self-synchronization loss: local audio and video features are cross-correlated over offsets, a softmax with temperature gives a distribution over offsets, and the loss is the negative log probability of zero offset. LatentSync \citep{li2024latentsync} carries SyncNet supervision into latent diffusion lip sync. These methods work on lips and speech with continuous outputs. PAL keeps the offset-distribution form of \citet{niu2023synchronization} and the frozen critic of \citet{prajwal2020wav2lip}, replaces the one-hot target with a window, and applies the loss to open-domain sound effects from a discrete-token generator.

\paragraph{Human audio-visual timing.}
Observers perceive auditory and visual signals as one event over a range of asynchronies, the temporal binding window \citep{wallace2014construct}. Audio lead is detected at smaller asynchronies than audio lag \citep{dixon1980detection}, and the window for audio-visual speech is wider on the audio-lag side \citep{vanwassenhove2007temporal,vroomen2010perception}. Its width differs across individuals \citep{stevenson2012individual}, and perceived simultaneity recalibrates after exposure to a constant lag \citep{fujisaki2004recalibration,takeshima2021visual}. We use these findings only to motivate the shape of the target over offsets.
